What the city reports say about Norco water

The City of Norco publishes an annual water quality report. In the 2022 data year it listed average hardness of about 122 mg/L as calcium carbonate, with a range of 54 to 290 mg/L. The 2021 data year showed an average of about 185 mg/L with a range of 21 to 220 mg/L.

The USGS calls 121 to 180 mg/L hard and over 180 very hard. Norco averages sit around those cutoffs. Water varies by where you live and which source feeds your street.

Softener or filter

They solve different problems. Many homes need one, some need both.

Water softener

  • Reduces hardness minerals.
  • Protects heaters and fixtures.
  • Needs salt or regeneration.
  • Does not remove chlorine.

Filtration

  • Reduces taste, chlorine and some contaminants.
  • Choose by what you want removed.
  • Needs filter changes.
  • Does not soften on its own.

A softener paired with a tankless water heater cuts descaling work.

Before you buy a system

A short checklist avoids buying the wrong equipment.

  • Test the hardness at your tap with a strip or ask a lab.
  • Read your utility annual water quality report.
  • Ask whether the home has a loop or bypass for a softener.
  • Check any local rules for brine discharge with your water utility.
  • Decide whether you want a whole-house or a single-tap system.

Salt, regeneration and what it takes to maintain one

A salt-based softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium and flushes them away in a regeneration cycle. It needs salt in the brine tank, a drain connection and power. Most owners refill salt every few weeks to months, depending on size and use.

Check with your water utility about any local rules on discharge, and ask your doctor if sodium is a concern. Potassium can replace sodium in many units.

Whole-house filtration

Carbon filters reduce chlorine taste and odor. Reverse osmosis at one tap reduces dissolved solids. Whole-house filters protect fixtures and appliances from sediment. Each solves a different problem, and the right choice follows your water test.

Ask your utility for its report first. It lists what is in the water at the source.

A note on honest expectations

A softener reduces hardness. It does not purify water, and it does not remove chlorine. It also will not fix a leak, a clog or a failing water heater by itself. It reduces scale, which can extend the life of heaters and fixtures.

Test your tap before you buy, and compare the result to your utility report. If your water is already on the soft side, a softener may not be worth it.

Code and permits

Softener installs follow the UPC-based California Plumbing Code for bypass valves, backflow and drain connections. Some cities also limit brine discharge, so ask the City of Norco first.
