When a sewer line fails and sewage backs up

A broken sewer line sends wastewater back into the lowest drains in your home. That is a health risk and a fast route to floor damage.

Stop all water use, keep people away from the affected area and open a window if the smell is strong. Do not use a rented drain machine on a line you suspect is broken, because it can snag and push roots or debris deeper.

Signs the line itself is damaged

Repeat backups

Clearing helps for days, then the clog returns.

Sewage odor outside

A smell in the yard can point to a leak in the line.

Sunken or soggy ground

A wet patch with no irrigation near it can be a break.

Multiple drains slow

More than one fixture backs up at the same time.

How we diagnose before we dig

We run a camera through the cleanout to see the pipe, mark the depth and locate problems. Cracks, offset joints, root intrusion and low spots show up clearly on video. We pair that with a locate on the surface so we know where the issue sits.

Our sewer inspection page explains what the camera shows and how to read the video. A good diagnosis lets us quote the repair accurately.

Roots, age and ground in Norco

Norco has a mix of older and newer properties. Norco was founded in 1923 and became a city in 1964, so some lines are many decades old. Clay and cast iron pipe from earlier eras can crack, offset or let roots in.

We do not guess your pipe type. The camera shows the material and the condition. Ask the City of Norco building department about permits for sewer lateral work, since requirements vary.

Trenchless lining in plain terms

A liner is a flexible sleeve soaked in resin that is pulled into the old pipe and cured. It forms a new pipe inside the old one and seals cracks and joints. It works when the old pipe is round and continuous. It does not fix a collapse or a deep belly.

Lining often means less digging and less damage to landscaping. The camera tells us if the pipe is a good candidate, and we tell you if it is not.

Who owns what

The lateral from your house to the main in the street is often your responsibility, at least up to a point. Where the boundary sits depends on the city and the sewer district. Ask the City of Norco public works department before you assume.

If the break is in the public main, we tell you and you report it to the city. We handle the private side.

Questions to ask before approving a sewer repair

Ask to see the camera video of the problem. Ask why the proposed method fits, what it costs if the scope changes and who handles the permit. Ask what the surface looks like when the work is finished.

You should leave with a plain description of the defect, the method and the price. If the plan changes in the trench, we call you before we continue.

Code and permits

Sewer repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, built on the UPC, covering pipe material, slope and cleanouts. Ask the City of Norco about permit and inspection rules before digging.
