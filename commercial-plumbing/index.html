Commercial plumbing emergencies after hours

A burst line, an overflowing floor drain or a dead water heater can close a business for the day.

If water is spreading, shut off the main valve and switch off power to wet areas. Tell staff and customers to stay clear of standing water, and photograph the damage for your records.

What to have ready when you call

Landlords and tenants often split plumbing responsibility. Having the facts ready speeds approval.

  • The building address and a contact on site.
  • Which fixtures or lines are affected.
  • Where the main shutoff and the cleanout are.
  • Whether you own the building or lease it, since the landlord may need to approve work.
  • Whether the problem is the building plumbing or tenant equipment.

Backflow, grease and compliance items

Some businesses need backflow device testing or a grease interceptor service. Rules vary by city and use, so ask the City of Norco and your water utility what applies to your site.

We can tell you what the work involves. We do not interpret local rules for you, since those come from the city and utility.

Restaurants and food service

Kitchen lines carry grease, starch and food solids, and they clog faster than a standard office line. Floor drains, prep sinks and dishwasher drains each need a plan. A grease interceptor needs regular service, and rules on that vary by city.

We help you find the cause of a recurring backup. Often it is a combination of a worn line and a cleaning schedule that is too far apart.

Tenant improvements and new builds

New restrooms, break rooms and equipment hookups start with a drain and supply plan. We review the space, rough in lines and coordinate with the general contractor. Inspections follow your city rules.

For a retail or office build-out, send us the plans or a sketch. A short review early avoids change orders later.

What a landlord and a tenant usually need to agree on

Leases often split plumbing duties. The landlord may own the building lines while the tenant owns equipment and fixtures inside the suite. Read the lease, or ask your property manager, before you authorize work.

We can explain what we found and where the line sits. We do not interpret your lease, but we write a clear description of the problem to share with whoever pays.

Code and permits

Commercial work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and many jobs need permits and inspections. We tell you which apply before we begin.
