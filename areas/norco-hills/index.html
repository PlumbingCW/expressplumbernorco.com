Where Norco Hills sits

Norco Hills is the name for the hillside neighborhoods on the southern side of Norco. The city uses the same name for a specific plan area, and a city park carries it too. The city does not publish one official boundary line for the neighborhood, so we describe it by what the planning documents say.

If you are not sure whether your street counts as Norco Hills, that is fine. Give us the street address when you call, and we confirm that we can help before anyone makes a plan.

What the city says about these neighborhoods

The City of Norco's draft Land Use and Community Character Assessment, dated November 6, 2023, describes the Norco Hills neighborhoods as sitting on the steeper slopes of the hills. It lists lots of 20,000 square feet or less, one- and two-story homes and cul-de-sacs.

The same document places these neighborhoods inside the Norco Hills and Norco Ridge Ranch specific plan areas and calls them newer than the original large-lot streets. It notes trails on both sides of the streets in the Norco Ridge Ranch plan.

Source type: City of Norco planning document, draft dated November 2023.

What listing sites add

Real-estate sites describe Norco Hills as a quiet area of larger homes with few sidewalks and horse trails. One of them reports a median build year of 2001. Another says many homes were built in 2000 or later, with some from the 1970s to the 1990s.

Treat those numbers as a rough guide. They come from listing data, not city records, and the sites draw their own boundaries. What it means for you: your home may be younger than the Norco average, but we still check the actual pipes and the actual water heater label.

Water pressure on a slope

Water pressure rises as the ground drops. The rule of thumb is about 0.43 psi for every foot of elevation, so a home at the low end of a slope can see much higher pressure than one near the top. We do not know the pressure on your street.

The California Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a pressure reducing valve where static pressure is above 80 psi. A cheap gauge on an outside hose bib tells you what you have. If the needle sits above 80, call and ask about a regulator before a supply line fails.

Big floor plans and slow hot water

A large home can put a long pipe run between the water heater and the farthest bathroom. Water in that pipe cools between uses, so you wait and waste water while the hot water arrives. A recirculation pump or a heater closer to the fixture can shorten the wait.

If you are weighing options, read our pages on water heater replacement and the tankless water heater before you decide. Sizing and venting matter more than the brand.

Water heater facts for California homes

Newer homes can still have an older heater, and a heater is the most common reason people in a newer area call a plumber. These are the facts worth checking first.

  • The U.S. Department of Energy puts the typical life of a storage tank heater at 10 to 15 years. Check the date on the label.
  • California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement. Ask whether yours is.
  • Most California cities require a permit for a water heater replacement. Ask the City of Norco building staff what applies to your address.
  • Close the cold water valve above a leaking heater and turn off the power or gas before you call.

City sewer or a private system: check, do not assume

We do not assume whether a given lot in Norco Hills connects to city sewer or uses a private system. Your utility bill is the quickest way to find out. The City of Norco Utilities office lists water, trash and sewer services and takes questions at (951) 270-5654.

Knowing this matters before any drain work. If a drain backs up again and again, a camera view of the line shows what is happening. See sewer inspection and drain cleaning for what that looks like.
