Norco Farms starts with a 1923 plan

The City of Norco says the town was launched in 1923 as a community of independent farmers. Developer Rex Clark ran it through the North Corona Land Company, and his plan included Norco Farms subdivisions around a village center. The city incorporated in 1964.

A century on, the streets of that plan hold homes from many decades, with additions, remodels and rebuilds. For plumbing, that means the age of the street tells you little. The age of your own pipes and heater is what counts, and we ask about it first.

How the city describes the area today

The City of Norco's draft Land Use and Community Character Assessment, dated November 6, 2023, describes the neighborhoods east of the I-15 freeway as the original large-lot neighborhoods. Most lots are over 20,000 square feet, most homes are one story and animal keeping is allowed. The streets were laid out in the 1920s and have no sidewalks.

The assessment bounds that area by the I-15 freeway on the west, River Drive and the Santa Ana River bluff on the north, and Hillside Drive and the Norco Hills on the east and south. The city does not label that area Norco Farms, so treat the match as a close fit, not an official boundary.

Source type: City of Norco planning document and city history page.

What listing sites report

Real-estate sites use Norco Farms as a neighborhood name. One reports a median build year near 1962 and a median lot size near 22,651 square feet, with one-story 1950s ranch homes common. Another says many homes were built between 1940 and 1969.

These figures come from listing data, not city records, and each site draws its own map. They are a reasonable guide to the mix. They do not tell us what pipes you have, so we never guess.

Questions to ask about a home from any decade

A home may have been repiped, extended or rebuilt, whatever its age. These questions replace guesswork with facts, and the answers help whoever works on the house.

  • How old is the water heater? The date is on the label, and tanks often run 10 to 15 years.
  • Where is the main shutoff, and does it turn all the way? Test it before you need it.
  • What pipe is visible under the sinks and at the heater? Photos help us a great deal on the phone.
  • Has anyone replaced or repaired the water or sewer lines, and was a permit pulled? Ask the seller or the previous owner.
  • Where is the sewer cleanout, and does a drain back up more than once a year?

Large lots mean long yard lines

A lot over 20,000 square feet is about half an acre. The pipe from the meter to the house can be long, and so can the drain from the house to the main. A leak in a long yard line may show up only as a high water bill or a wet patch.

Ask the City of Norco which part of the line belongs to you. Then see water line repair and leak detection for how a hidden yard leak is found. If a drain keeps backing up, read about sewer inspection.

Horse properties and outdoor water

Norco keeps about 140 miles of horse trails, according to the city, and many properties around Norco Farms keep animals. Outdoor spigots, trough lines and wash racks add water points that a town lot does not have.

Hose connections are expected to have a vacuum breaker, so water cannot siphon back into the supply. A spigot that drips or sprays from the handle is worth a look. See faucet repair for the outdoor fixtures we service.

Nearby places we also help

The Santa Ana River runs along the northern side of this area, and Eastvale sits across it. Our Eastvale page covers that city. The newer hillside streets to the south are on our Norco Hills page.

The city's planning assessment also mentions SilverLakes Sports Park, a 130-acre city-owned venue. It is a park, not a neighborhood, so ask us if you are unsure about your address.
