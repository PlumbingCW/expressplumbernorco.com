Plumbing installation for Norco homes and additions

New plumbing is easier to plan than to repair in a hurry. Whether you are adding a bathroom, moving a kitchen sink or finishing a laundry room, the rough-in decides how well everything works for decades.

We install fixtures, supply lines, drains, shutoff valves and appliance connections. For leaks and failures, see leak detection and burst pipe repair instead.

Permits and code in California

California plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code. New work often needs a permit and an inspection, but rules vary by city and scope. Ask the City of Norco building department what your project needs.

You can check any plumber on the Contractors State License Board lookup before you hire. We encourage it.

Hard water and new fixtures in Norco

City reports put Norco water hardness at roughly 120 to 185 mg/L in recent data years. Scale shortens the life of faucets, valves and water heaters. Consider shutoff valves at every fixture so future repairs are easy.

If you are installing a new water heater or fixtures with fine screens, a water softener helps. We can plan the loop during the install.

Rough-in basics

The rough-in is the plumbing inside the walls and under the floor before fixtures go on. Drain pipes need the correct slope and venting. Supply lines need shutoffs and the right size for the fixture load.

Mistakes at this stage are expensive to fix after the drywall goes up. We pressure test supply lines and water test drains before inspection and before the walls close.

Fixtures that need extra planning

Freestanding tubs, wall-mounted toilets, tankless water heaters and undermount sinks all have special needs. Ask us about them early, since the framing or cabinetry may need changes.

Bring the fixture spec sheets to the planning visit. They list the required rough-in dimensions and connections.

Shutoff valves at every fixture

A shutoff valve under each sink and behind each toilet lets you stop one fixture without turning off the whole house. It makes future repairs faster and cheaper, and it limits damage when a supply line fails.

It is a small addition to any installation. We include working shutoffs on the fixtures we set, and we show you where each one is.

Code and permits

New fixtures and rough-in work follow the UPC-based California Plumbing Code, including fixture spacing, venting and shutoff valves. Larger installs generally need a permit.
