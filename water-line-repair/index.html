Water line leak: stop it and call

A leak in the line from the meter to your house wastes water and can soak the yard or undermine a driveway. Close the shutoff at the meter or at the house if you can reach it. Call us.

If the shutoff does not hold, call your water utility, because the meter and the street side belong to them. We handle the line on your side.

Signs of a service line leak

Wet or soft ground

A patch that stays wet with the sprinklers off.

Low pressure at every tap

The whole house loses pressure.

Meter keeps moving

With all fixtures off, the dial or indicator turns.

High bill

Usage rises with no change in habits.

Long lines on Norco properties

Norco has strong equestrian roots, with 140 miles of horse trails listed by the city. Properties with room for horses and outbuildings can have long runs between the meter and the house, plus extra lines to barns and troughs.

More pipe means more places to leak. We trace the line, mark the leak and recommend a repair or a replacement. Homes on slopes can also see high water pressure, which our Norco Hills page explains.

Where Norco water comes from, and why it matters

The City of Norco reports about 83% of its 2023 supply was purchased treated groundwater, with the rest from city wells. Source mix can change in droughts or emergencies.

Pressure and chemistry vary by street. We test pressure at the house and recommend a regulator if it is high, since high pressure strains service lines.

Know your shutoff and your meter

There are usually two shutoffs. One is at the meter, near the street, and belongs to the utility. The other is at the house. Locate both now. A meter shutoff often needs a special key, so ask your utility if you cannot turn it.

Label the house shutoff so anyone in your home can find it fast.

Pressure regulators protect the line

A regulator near the house reduces street pressure to a safe level. When the regulator fails, pressure can spike and strain the service line and fixtures. A gauge on a hose bib tells you what pressure you have.

Ask us to check pressure whenever a line leak is repaired. A leak sometimes points to a pressure problem upstream.

Questions to ask about a service line replacement

Ask what pipe material is used, how deep it goes and whether the trench or the trenchless method fits your yard. Ask who handles the permit and the inspection, and what restoration is included.

Ask for a clear price before work starts. A water line job disturbs the yard, so agree on what the surface looks like when we are done.

Code and permits

Service line work follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC, and may need a city permit and inspection. Digging also requires an 811 call so utility lines are marked.
