Toilet overflow: stop the water first

An overflowing toilet is urgent because water spreads fast. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the flow, or close the supply valve at the wall by turning it clockwise. Then call us.

Do not keep flushing. If other drains gurgle too, the clog may be past the toilet. In that case, see drain cleaning.

Why toilets fail in Norco homes

Chlorine in treated drinking water wears rubber over time. The Norco water report lists chlorine as the disinfectant in the system, with levels averaging about 1.6 mg/L in the 2022 data. Rubber flappers soften and leak after a few years.

Hard water, between roughly 120 and 185 mg/L in the city reports, also clogs fill valves and rim jets with scale. If your toilet runs after you replace the flapper, scale is the next place to look.

Repair or replace the toilet

Repair when

  • The toilet is less than 15 years old.
  • The problem is a flapper, fill valve or seal.
  • The bowl and tank have no cracks.
  • Flushing is otherwise strong.

Replace when

  • The bowl or tank is cracked.
  • The toilet clogs weekly.
  • Parts are no longer available.
  • You want a more efficient model.

If you want a new toilet, we also handle installation. See plumbing installation for fixtures and fittings.

Quick checks before you call

These checks help us bring the right parts.

  • Look at the tank. Is water running over the overflow tube?
  • Drop food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means a leaking flapper.
  • Check the supply line for drips.
  • Press down on the bowl to see if it rocks.
  • Look for stains on the floor near the base.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet is a reasonable fix for a cracked bowl or a model that clogs weekly. We remove the old unit, check the flange, set a new seal and bolt the toilet down level. We test the flush and the supply line before we leave.

Pick a toilet with a rough-in that matches your drain, which is the distance from the wall to the center of the flange. Twelve inches is common, and we check yours.

Water use and efficiency

Modern toilets use less water per flush than older ones. A running toilet can waste far more than a good flush uses. Fixing a flapper often saves more water than replacing a sound toilet.

If your bill rose recently, a silent leak in a toilet is the first place to look. The dye test in the tank takes a quarter of an hour.

Signs a toilet is wasting water silently

A toilet that refills by itself every few minutes, a faint hiss in the bathroom or a damp spot on the ceiling below are clues. The tank may be leaking to the bowl, or the fill valve may not seal.

Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look at the bowl. Colored water means the flapper is leaking and needs replacing.

Code and permits

Replacement toilets must meet water use limits set in California law, and installation follows the UPC-based California Plumbing Code. A simple part swap needs neither.
