Slab leak emergency: what to do right now

A leak under the slab can erode soil, damage flooring and run up your bill. Shut off the main water valve and call us.

If water is rising through the floor or a wall, switch off the breaker to the affected area. Do not walk on wet floors near outlets.

Signs of a slab leak

Warm spots on the floor

A hot water line leaking under concrete can warm the surface.

Hissing or running water sound

Heard with everything off.

Unexplained bill increase

Water is escaping before it shows.

Cracks or damp carpet

Moisture finds its way up through cracks.

Insurance and what to document

Some homeowner policies cover sudden water damage and the cost of accessing the leak, while others exclude slab leaks. Ask your insurer before work starts.

We write down what we found and where. Take photos of damage, keep water bills and note when you first noticed symptoms.

Norco housing and slab leaks

Many Southern California homes sit on concrete slabs, and copper pipes under the slab can wear over decades. Norco became a city in 1964, so some homes are old enough for aging pipe.

Hard water at about 120 to 185 mg/L in city reports speeds pipe wall wear. We check your pipe material and pressure when we locate the leak.

After the repair

We pressure test the repaired line and run water long enough to confirm the leak is gone. Ask the contractor who restores the floor to wait until the test passes. Keep your water bills for the next two cycles so you can confirm usage is back to normal.

If a slab leak happened once, other lines of the same age may be weakening too. Ask us whether a partial repipe would help.

Preventing the next one

High water pressure speeds pipe wear. A regulator and a gauge at the hose bib show where you stand. Water chemistry matters too, since hard water builds scale and corrosion can attack copper from the outside or the inside.

Know where your main shutoff is and test it twice a year. A valve that does not close cannot save you in a leak.

Why we locate before we cut

Concrete is expensive to open and to patch. A wrong guess means a second hole and a second repair bill. Locating with listening devices and thermal tools keeps the opening small and in the right place.

It also shows whether one line or several are leaking. That decides whether a spot repair makes sense or a reroute is the better call.

Code and permits

Slab repairs and reroutes follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC. A reroute through walls or attic can need a permit, which we raise in the plan.
