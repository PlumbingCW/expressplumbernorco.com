Tankless water heaters in Norco homes

A tankless unit heats water as it flows instead of storing it. That saves floor space and can cut standby heat loss. It also puts more load on gas lines, vents and the unit itself.

We install, repair and flush tankless heaters. If your current tank has failed, our water heater replacement page covers emergency swaps, including a move to tankless.

Signs your tankless unit needs service

Error codes

Many units flash a code for scale, flame loss or low flow.

Water turns lukewarm

Scale on the heat exchanger cuts output.

Short bursts of cold

Sensor or flow problems cause cold sandwiches mid-shower.

Noise or clicking

Ignition issues or vent restrictions can sound like clicking or rumbling.

Flushing in Norco water

Norco city reports show average hardness of roughly 120 to 185 mg/L as calcium carbonate in recent years. Hard water leaves scale inside a tankless heat exchanger. Scale reduces flow and triggers error codes.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit with a small pump, then rinses it. Many makers suggest yearly service in hard water. Check your manual and ask us how often fits your home. A water softener reduces the scale load.

What we check at an install visit

A good install starts with measurements, not the product box.

  • Gas line size and run length for the total load.
  • Venting path, clearances and condensate drain if the unit needs one.
  • Water pressure and flow at your busiest fixtures.
  • Whether you need isolation valves for future flushing.
  • Any electrical outlet or circuit the unit requires.

Sizing a tankless unit for the way you live

Tankless units are rated by flow, not by gallons stored. Add up the fixtures that run together at your busiest time, such as a shower and a dishwasher, and match that to the unit rating at your winter inlet temperature. An undersized unit gives lukewarm water when two fixtures run.

If your home is large or has a soaking tub, we may suggest two smaller units or a larger single model. We size by use, not by the number of bathrooms.

Gas line and venting basics

A tankless unit burns a lot of gas for a short time. Old half-inch gas lines may not carry enough, and a longer run needs a larger pipe. Venting goes through a wall or roof with the right clearances, and some high-efficiency models drain condensate that must go to a proper drain.

These are the parts that surprise homeowners on price. We check them at the start and tell you if the gas line or vent will cost more than the unit.

When tankless is not the right call

A tankless unit is a poor fit when the gas line cannot carry the load without a costly upgrade, when there is no good place to vent, or when the household uses very little hot water. A good tank can cost less overall in those cases.

We tell you plainly when a tank makes more sense. Our goal is the right unit for your home, not the most expensive one.

Code and permits

Tankless units must meet the manufacturer instructions and the UPC-based California Plumbing Code for venting and gas sizing. Both limits can drive the cost more than the unit itself.
