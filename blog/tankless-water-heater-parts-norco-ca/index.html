Tankless Water Heater Parts in Norco, CA: Homeowner Guide

A plain guide to the parts inside a tankless water heater and which ones wear out first.

By the Norco Express Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Talk to a plumber
(951) 467-1450
Call Now

The short answer: scale causes most tankless part problems

A tankless water heater has a small number of parts that matter. The heat exchanger, flow sensor, igniter or elements, inlet filter and control board do most of the work. In hard water, scale on the heat exchanger and sensors causes more trouble than any single part failing on its own.

If your unit shows an error code, check the manual for the meaning first. Many codes point to flow, ignition or temperature sensors. A flush solves a good share of them.

Main parts and what they do

PartWhat it doesCommon problem
Heat exchangerTransfers heat from the burner or elements to the waterScale buildup, reduced output.
Flow sensorTells the unit when water is runningClogs, unit will not fire.
Inlet filter screenCatches debris before it enters the unitClogged, low flow.
Igniter or flame sensorStarts and monitors the flame in a gas unitIgnition failure, error codes.
Temperature sensorsMeasure inlet and outlet water temperatureWrong readings, cold bursts.
Control boardRuns the unitFault codes, no power.
Isolation valvesLet you flush the unitMissing on older installs.

How a flush works on a tankless unit

  1. Isolate

    Close the supply valves and open the service ports.

  2. Circulate

    A small pump moves descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

  3. Rinse

    Flush with clean water until it runs clear.

  4. Restore

    Open valves, clean the inlet filter and test the unit.

Parts you can check, and parts to leave to a plumber

You can clean an inlet filter if you know where the shutoffs are. Check the manual for the location. Gas parts, venting and the heat exchanger are for a licensed plumber, since a mistake can cause a gas leak or carbon monoxide risk.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911. Do not try to find the leak yourself.

Hardness and your tankless unit in Norco

City of Norco reports list system hardness at roughly 122 mg/L in the 2022 data and roughly 185 mg/L in the 2021 data. Most tankless makers recommend regular flushing in hard water, and the interval depends on the model.

Check your manual for the interval and ask your water utility for its latest annual report. A water softener ahead of the unit reduces scale at the source.

Parts questions, answered honestly

Parts are brand specific. A flow sensor from one maker will not fit another. We do not sell generic parts on a guess, and we read the model label first.

If a heat exchanger has failed on an older unit, replacement of the whole unit may cost less than the part. Our water heater repair page explains how we decide.

A basic flush to remove scale

  1. Shut off

    Turn off power and gas, then close the water valves.

  2. Connect

    Attach hoses to the service valves and a pump to a bucket of descaling solution.

  3. Circulate

    Run the solution through the unit for the time the maker lists.

  4. Rinse

    Flush with clean water, clean the filter and reopen the valves.

Follow the maker manual for your model, since each unit lists its own flush steps.

When a part is not the real problem

Error codes often point to a symptom, not a broken part. A flow error may come from a clogged filter, a closed valve or low supply pressure. Replacing the sensor first wastes money, so check the simple causes before you buy anything.

A plumber can read the code, test the part and tell you whether repair or replacement makes more sense. If the unit is old and parts are scarce, a new unit may cost less over time than a major repair.

Repair versus replace: a practical rule

If the unit is still under a maker warranty or is only a few years old, repair usually makes sense. If the heat exchanger has failed on an older unit, the repair can cost a large part of a new one. Ask for both prices side by side.

Parts availability matters too. A plumber can tell you whether parts for your model are still made. A unit that cannot be repaired is not worth a long diagnosis.

Keep a record of the unit

Write down the model, the serial number and the install date, and photograph the rating plate. Add each flush and service visit. With a short record, any plumber can see the history, check the warranty and find the right part quickly.

Water quality and the inlet filter

The inlet filter is the cheapest part to maintain and the one most often ignored. Debris from the supply line collects there and starves the unit of flow. Check it when you flush, and clean it with a soft brush and clear water.

City reports show hard water in Norco, so scale is a real factor. A scheduled flush is cheaper than a heat exchanger repair, and it also helps the unit hold its rated output. Ask your utility for its latest hardness figure to plan the schedule.

Ask before you buy a part online

Parts sold online may fit a similar model but not yours. Match the full model number, not just the brand and the capacity. A wrong sensor can trigger new error codes and leave you with two problems instead of one.

Code note

Parts must match the maker listing, and venting and gas work follow the UPC-based California Plumbing Code.
