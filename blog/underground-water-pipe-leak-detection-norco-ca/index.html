Underground Water Pipe Leak Detection in Norco, CA

How to tell if a buried pipe is leaking, how plumbers find the spot and what repair involves.

By the Norco Express Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Talk to a plumber
(951) 467-1450
Call Now

The short answer: confirm the leak with your meter, then locate it

A leak in a buried water line often hides. The signs are a bill that climbs for no reason, a soft or green patch in the yard, the sound of water when everything is off and a meter that keeps moving. The first step is a simple meter test, which takes about thirty minutes.

Once the meter confirms a leak, a plumber narrows it down with listening gear and pressure tests. Digging comes last, and it should happen in the right spot the first time.

The meter test you can do yourself

  1. Shut everything off

    Stop all water use inside and outside, including ice makers and irrigation.

  2. Read the meter

    Find the leak indicator, usually a small triangle or dial, and note it or take a photo.

  3. Wait

    Leave the water off for thirty minutes or more.

  4. Check again

    If the dial moved or the numbers rose, water is going somewhere.

Is it the house line or the street side

Your property line runs from the meter to the house. The utility owns the meter and usually the pipe up to it. The homeowner usually owns the line after the meter. Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house valve.

If the meter stops when the house valve closes, the leak is inside the home or past the valve. That helps you decide whether the work belongs in the yard or in the walls.

Signs of a leak in a buried line

These clues are worth checking soon, because a small leak grows over time.

  • A wet or sunken patch in the yard when it has not rained.
  • Green, lush growth in one strip while the rest is dry.
  • Unexplained drops in water pressure.
  • The sound of running water when all fixtures are off.
  • A bill that rises for no clear reason.

Norco lots, utilities and who to ask

The City of Norco operates its own water system, so questions about the meter and the main belong with the city. Ask the city who owns the line at your address and whether the meter box can be opened by a plumber. The line from the meter to the house is generally your responsibility.

Properties with a long run from the meter to the house or to an outbuilding have more to test. A longer run means more pipe to test and more places for a leak. Ask the plumber to test each branch separately.

Ask the City of Norco for your average usage history. A step up in usage on the bill tells you roughly when the leak started.

Repair options for a buried line

A short leak can be fixed with a spot repair. The plumber opens the ground over the leak, cuts out the damaged piece and joins new pipe with proper fittings. Trenchless methods can replace a longer run with less yard damage, but they need the right conditions.

If the pipe is old, brittle or leaking in several places, replacing the full run is often better than patching it again. Ask for a price on both options, and ask what the yard will look like when the work is done.

Call before you dig

Before any digging, call 811 so utility lines get marked. In California, this is required for most digging jobs. A plumber should handle that step, but if you plan to dig your own yard, make the call yourself at least two working days ahead.

What a leak costs you if you wait

Water under the ground softens soil and can undermine slabs, patios and driveways. It wastes water you pay for, and it can raise your bill month after month. A leak that you fix early costs one repair. A leak that you ignore can turn into a damaged driveway and a bigger repair.

If you see water pooling or the ground sinking, shut the water at the house valve and call. Our leak detection team finds leaks without guesswork. We serve Norco, Corona and Eastvale.

What the visit looks like

On arrival, the plumber asks about your bill, the meter reading and what you have noticed. They check the meter, close valves to split the line into sections and listen along the path. The plumber then marks the spot on the ground so you can see where the opening will be.

Before any work, you get the finding, the repair options and the price. If you only want the leak located, you can stop there and use the information to get other quotes. Our water line repair team handles the fix when you are ready.

Pressure and your supply line

High household pressure strains buried joints and fixtures. A simple gauge on a hose bib shows your static pressure. If the reading is high, a pressure regulator near the house shutoff protects the line and the fixtures. Ask a plumber to check it during the visit.
