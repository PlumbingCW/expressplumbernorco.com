Garbage Disposal Repair in Norco, CA: Fix or Replace

A practical guide to jammed, noisy and leaking disposals, and how to decide between repair and replacement.

By the Norco Express Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Talk to a plumber

The short answer: a hum means a jam, a leak means replace

A disposal that hums and does not spin is jammed. A disposal that leaks from the housing is finished. A disposal that does nothing may only need the reset button. Most problems fall into one of those three groups.

Always cut power first. Flip the breaker or unplug the unit, and never reach into the chamber with your hand.

How to free a jam safely

  1. Power off

    Unplug the unit or turn off the breaker.

  2. Look

    Use a flashlight and tongs, not your hand.

  3. Turn

    Put a hex key in the bottom socket and work it back and forth.

  4. Reset

    Press the red reset button, restore power and run cold water.

Why disposals fail

Most disposals fail from jams, overload or age. Hard objects, fibrous stalks and large amounts of grease are common causes. The motor runs hot when it strains, which wears the seals and bearings.

A disposal usually lasts several years, but use and care vary. A unit that has leaked once will leak again, so treat a leak as the end of its life.

Hard water and the kitchen line in Norco

City of Norco water reports put hardness between about 122 and 185 mg/L in recent data years. That is enough to leave scale on the drain line below the disposal.

Scale narrows the pipe and snags food. If your sink slows even with the disposal off, the problem is the line. Ask about drain cleaning rather than a new disposal.

Ask your water utility for its latest annual report to see your street area.

Habits that extend disposal life

These habits cost nothing and help.

  • Run cold water before, during and after grinding.
  • Feed small amounts, not a full sink at once.
  • Skip bones, pits, fibrous stalks and large amounts of coffee grounds.
  • Keep grease out of the sink entirely.
  • Grind ice and citrus now and then to clean the chamber.

Repair or replace

Repair when the problem is a jam, a reset or a flange seal. Replace when the housing leaks, the motor will not run or the unit is old and noisy. A new unit is a simple swap on the same mount in many sinks.

We handle both. If your sink is backed up, we also serve Corona and Eastvale.

Installing a replacement unit: what matters

Match the horsepower to how you cook. A smaller motor handles light daily use, and a larger motor handles heavy use with fewer jams. Check that the mounting kit fits your sink and that the discharge pipe lines up with your trap.

Many replacements reuse the old mounting ring, which makes the swap quick. Others need a new flange and fresh plumber putty under the sink opening. Always run the new unit with water and look for drips for several minutes before you close the cabinet.

Wiring and the dishwasher connection

Many disposals hard-wire to a switch or plug into an outlet under the sink. If yours is hard-wired, treat the electrical side as a job for a qualified person. Your dishwasher drain usually connects to the disposal inlet, so knock out the plug on a new unit before you hook the dishwasher up, or it will not drain.

Odors and what to do about them

A smell that returns after cleaning usually means food residue on the underside of the splash guard or in the grinding chamber. Scrub the rubber guard with a brush and dish soap, then grind a handful of ice and a citrus peel to scrape the walls.

A smell that does not go away may come from a partial clog in the trap or the drain line. If the sink also drains slowly, the problem is below the disposal, and cleaning the line will help more than replacing the unit.

Keep the manual and the model number

The model number on the underside of the unit tells a plumber the mount type and the wiring. Photograph it before it gets buried behind storage. Reset buttons and wrench sockets differ by brand, so the manual saves guesswork.

Know your breaker and your switch

Find the breaker that feeds the disposal and label it. In an emergency, you will want to cut power in seconds. If the wall switch feels hot or the breaker trips each time you use the unit, stop and have it checked.

Code note

Drain connections follow the UPC-based California Plumbing Code, while wiring follows separate electrical rules.
