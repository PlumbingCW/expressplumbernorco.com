Anti-Siphon Faucet Repair in Norco, CA: A Homeowner Guide

What an anti-siphon spigot does, why it leaks and how to repair or replace it.

By the Norco Express Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Talk to a plumber
(951) 467-1450
Call Now

The short answer: the vacuum breaker is the likely culprit

An anti-siphon faucet has a vacuum breaker on top. It lets air into the pipe when the water is off so hose water cannot flow back into your house supply. When it fails, you see a spray or a drip from the top while the faucet runs.

The fix is often a new vacuum breaker or a new faucet. A leak at the handle is a different problem, usually packing or an O-ring.

Behind the wall or insideFailed pipe or fittingCall a plumber.

Why backflow protection matters

A hose left in a bucket, a pool or a spray bottle of chemicals can siphon liquid back into the house line when pressure drops. That contaminates your supply. A vacuum breaker blocks that path.

Some jobs call for stronger protection, like fertilizer injectors or irrigation lines. A pressure vacuum breaker or a double check valve is the usual device. Ask a plumber what suits your layout.

Replacing the vacuum breaker or the faucet

  1. Shut off

    Close the line valve inside the house or at the main.

  2. Remove

    Unscrew the old breaker or unthread the faucet from the pipe.

  3. Clean

    Clear old tape and scale from the threads.

  4. Install

    Wrap threads, seat the new part and open the water slowly.

If the pipe twists or the wall leaks while you work, stop and call. A cracked pipe inside a wall is a plumber job.

Norco notes: hoses, water and backflow rules

Norco properties with animals and outbuildings often have several hose bibs, which raises the chance that one is left with a hose attached. Remove hoses when not in use to protect the faucet and the supply.

Backflow rules for irrigation and larger properties are set by the water utility. The City of Norco operates its own water utility, so ask the city what your property needs.

City of Norco reports show hardness near 122 to 185 mg/L in recent years. Scale can clog a vacuum breaker, so rinse or replace it when it starts to spit.

Source type: City of Norco water system page and water quality reports.

Quick care steps

A few habits make outdoor faucets last.

  • Disconnect hoses when the faucet is not in use.
  • Open the faucet slowly to avoid pressure shock.
  • Do not leave a hose in a bucket or pool.
  • Look at the top of the faucet for white scale.
  • Replace brittle hose bibs before they fail.

When to call a plumber

Call when the faucet is loose in the wall, when a leak appears behind the wall or when you want irrigation backflow protection installed. Our faucet repair team also fixes indoor and outdoor valves.

How a vacuum breaker actually works

Inside the cap is a small disc held up by water pressure. When the faucet is on, the disc seals the vent and water flows to the hose. When the faucet shuts, pressure drops, the disc falls and the vent opens. Air enters the pipe, and nothing can be pulled backward from the hose.

That vent is why a few drops may fall from the top when you shut the faucet off. A short drip as the water drains is normal. A steady spray while the water runs is not, and it means the disc or seat is worn or fouled with scale.

Diagnosing a leak in ten minutes

  1. Disconnect the hose

    A kinked or full hose can push water out of the wrong place.

  2. Run it dry first

    Open the faucet with nothing attached and watch where water appears.

  3. Check the stem

    If water seeps around the handle, the packing nut or O-ring is the cause, not the breaker.

  4. Inspect the cap

    Look for white crust or cracks on the vent cap. Crust points to scale and cracks point to replacement.

Repair or replace the whole spigot

SituationBetter choiceWhy
Only the cap is cracked or crustedReplace the vacuum breakerThe body is still sound, so the swap is cheap and quick.
Stem leaks and the faucet is oldReplace the spigotWorn brass and a failing seat tend to return.

What to avoid

Do not plug or seal the vent on the cap to stop the spray. That defeats the backflow protection and can send hose water into the supply. Do not use pipe tools that crush the thin brass tube behind the faucet either, because the tube is the part you cannot see.

Seasonal habits for Norco yards

Hose bibs see the most use in the warm months, when irrigation, car washing and pet areas run daily. Brass fittings that sit in the sun and spray get brittle, and hoses left screwed on trap pressure against the vent. Unscrew the hose after each use and the faucet lasts longer.

If you run drip irrigation or a timer, look at the connection every few weeks. A small drip at a timer fitting wastes water and can hide a failing vacuum breaker behind it.

What to have ready when you call

A few details let the plumber bring the right part the first time.

  • Where the water appears: the cap, the stem or the spout.
  • Whether a hose or timer is attached.
  • Whether the leak is constant or only when the water runs.
  • A photo of the faucet and the wall around it.
  • The location of your main shutoff valve.

A note on frost and old brass

Norco winters are mild, but older brass faucets still crack from age, not only from cold. If the body shows green corrosion or a hairline split, replace the whole spigot instead of the cap. A cracked body can fail without warning when a hose is under pressure.

Code note

Backflow protection on hose bibs is required by the UPC-based California Plumbing Code.
