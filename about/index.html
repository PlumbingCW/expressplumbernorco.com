Who we are

Norco Express Plumbing is a plumbing information and enquiry site for Norco, CA. Call and describe your problem, and we help you work out the next step. The pages cover Norco, Eastvale and Corona.

The site is built around one idea: when water is where it should not be, you want a clear plan fast. Each page tells you what the job involves and what to ask before you agree to work.

What we handle

Emergency work comes first on this site, because that is when people need help most. That means burst pipes, sewer backups, leaking water heaters and hidden leaks that show up as a wet floor or a high bill.

Planned work matters just as much. This site explains water heater replacement, new supply lines, fixture installs and drain cleaning before they fail. Homes and small commercial spaces are both covered, and commercial plumbing has its own page.

How a call works

Every call starts with questions. What do you see, where is the shutoff, and how old is the home? Those answers help us bring the right parts and explain the job in plain terms.

Ask for a price before work starts. If the job changes once a wall or a trench is open, ask to be called before the work continues. You decide, and you should hear the reason.

From call to finished repair

  1. Call

    You describe the problem and your address.

  2. Stabilize

    We tell you how to shut off water or power and limit damage.

  3. Ask

    Ask for the scope and the price before anyone starts.

  4. Repair

    The problem is fixed, tested and explained to you.

Local knowledge

Norco water is moderately hard to hard in the city reports, with averages near 122 mg/L in the 2022 data year and near 185 mg/L in 2021. That scale shortens the life of heaters and fixtures, so we ask about it on water heater and fixture jobs.

Norco was founded in 1923 and became a city in 1964. Older homes can carry older pipe and fittings, so ask about pipe age before any repair. Each service and city page on this site explains the local reasons behind the work.

Safety comes before speed

Some problems are not for a plumber alone. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. If water is near electrical outlets or panels, stay out of the area and turn off power at the breaker only if the floor is dry and the panel is dry.

The shutoff and safety steps on this site are general. Follow your utility's advice first. A fast repair is worth little if someone gets hurt while waiting for it.

Residential and commercial work

Most questions come from homeowners, and the work should be planned around a household that has to keep using the kitchen and bathrooms. Small businesses ask about drains, restrooms, water heaters and leaks that stop them from opening.

For a business, ask about hours, who owns the pipe and whether a landlord must approve the work. Ask for a plain description of the problem that you can share with whoever pays for it.

After the repair

When the work is done, ask to see it tested. Ask where the shutoff is, what was replaced and what to look for if the problem returns. Take photos of the finished work and keep the paperwork with your home records.

If a plumber sees something else that needs attention, such as an aging water heater or a corroded valve, you should be told and the decision should stay with you. Nothing gets added to the job without your say.

Checking any plumber before you hire

Before you hire us or anyone else, check the contractor license with the California Contractors State License Board online lookup. You can search by license number or by business name, and it shows whether the license is active. This site does not display a license number, so ask whoever does the work for theirs.

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and some jobs need permits. Ask for a written scope and a price. Ask what happens if the job changes. A good plumber answers plainly and does not push you to decide in a hurry.

What to expect from any good plumber

  • A phone call that starts with questions.
  • A written scope and price before work starts.
  • Plain explanations of what failed and why.
  • Clean work areas and cleanup after the job.
  • Honest advice, including when a repair is not worth the money.

What we do not do

This site does not promise an arrival time, does not quote a price over the phone for a problem nobody has seen and does not publish made-up reviews or ratings. If a claim cannot be checked, it does not belong on this site.
