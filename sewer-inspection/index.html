Why scope a sewer line

A sewer camera gives you facts. It shows roots, cracks, offset joints, low spots and buildup that no one can see from the surface. You use that information before a purchase, after a backup or before major yard work.

This page covers planned inspections. If sewage is backing up now, see sewer line repair, which covers emergency service.

What the inspection involves

  1. Access

    We find the cleanout or pull a toilet to reach the line.

  2. Scope

    A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels the pipe.

  3. Locate

    We mark depth and position at the surface.

  4. Report

    We explain findings and share the video.

Before you buy a home

A home inspector may not scope the sewer line. Asking for a camera inspection during the buying period can reveal costly problems before you close. Findings give you facts to negotiate on repairs or price.

We provide the video and a plain-language summary. We do not give legal or real estate advice, so share the results with your agent.

Norco properties and lateral lines

Norco has a strong equestrian heritage, with 140 miles of horse trails listed by the city. Properties with room for horses and outbuildings can have longer lateral runs, which means more pipe to inspect, and trees near the line raise the chance of roots.

If the home has been around since the city was incorporated in 1964, an inspection makes more sense. Ask the City of Norco about records for your lateral.

Reading the video with us

We narrate what the camera shows, such as the pipe material, joints and any buildup. You can see a root ball, a crack or a sag as it appears. We pause on anything that needs a closer look and measure the distance from the cleanout.

The depth and distance help us quote a repair. They also tell you whether a problem sits under the driveway or the lawn.

What a camera cannot tell you

A camera shows the inside of the pipe. It cannot tell you how much longer the pipe will last, and it cannot see beyond a full blockage. If a line is too clogged to pass, we clear it first.

It also does not replace a city or lender requirement. If a buyer, lender or the city needs a specific report, ask what format they accept.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone to scope a line

Ask whether you will get the video, whether the plumber will mark depth and location, and whether the price includes a written summary. Ask what happens if the line is too clogged for the camera to pass.

A good inspection leaves you with evidence you can share with a buyer, a seller or a repair contractor. A quick look with no record helps no one later.

Code and permits

A camera inspection needs no permit. If it shows a defect, the repair falls under the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC, and the video helps document the condition.
