When a faucet will not shut off

A faucet that will not turn off wastes water and can flood a cabinet or sink. Close the angle stop valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If there are none, shut the main valve to the house.

Then call us. A valve that will not close often needs replacement, which is a quick job once we have the right part.

Common faucet problems

Drip from the spout

A worn cartridge, washer or seat is the usual cause.

Leak at the handle

Packing nut or O-ring wear lets water seep out.

Low flow

Scale clogs the aerator or cartridge.

Wobbly base

Loose mounting can leak underneath.

Hard water and faucets in Norco

Scale from hard water is the most common cause of faucet trouble. City of Norco reports put average hardness at roughly 120 to 185 mg/L in recent data years, which is enough to coat aerators and cartridges.

A white crust on a faucet is a clue. Soaking the aerator in vinegar may help short term. A water softener reduces the buildup across the house.

Before you call

A few quick checks save time on the visit.

  • Find the angle stops under the sink and try to close them.
  • Take a photo of the faucet and any brand name.
  • Note whether it leaks hot, cold or both.
  • Check for water under the sink.
  • Remove the aerator and look for debris.

Choosing a replacement faucet

A new faucet is worth considering when the finish is worn, the body is corroded or the cartridge is discontinued. Match the hole count in your sink and the supply connections. Buy from a maker with replacement parts available for years.

Single-handle faucets have fewer parts than two-handle models. Pull-down sprayers save time at the kitchen sink and need a clear space below for the hose.

Outdoor spigots

Hose bibs take a beating from sun and pressure. Leaks at the handle often need new packing. A spray from the top means the vacuum breaker has failed on an anti-siphon spigot. That part prevents hose water from flowing back into the house supply.

A hose left connected can trap pressure and strain the line. Disconnect hoses when not in use.

Why a drip is worth fixing

A faucet that drips once a second wastes thousands of gallons over a year, and the water has to be paid for. The drip also stains the sink and points to a worn part that will fail completely sooner or later.

Fixing it early usually costs a cartridge or a washer. Waiting until the valve will not close turns a small repair into a same-night call.

Keep the old part when you can. A plumber can match a cartridge or a valve seat by sight in seconds, and a photo of the faucet base and the brand name helps too.

Code and permits

Faucets and supply lines fall under the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC, which also covers backflow protection on outdoor spigots. Simple repairs rarely need a permit.
